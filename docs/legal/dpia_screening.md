# Screening for a data protection impact assessment (GDPR Article 35)

RightFit online. 20 Nov 2026.

## The question

Is the processing likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, so that an impact assessment is required (Article 35(1))? Checked against Article 35(3), the criteria of the European Data Protection Board's guidelines (WP248 rev.01) and the list published by the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos.

| Criterion | Present? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation or scoring of persons, profiling | no | the checks evaluate documents and products, not people |
| Automated decisions with legal or similar effect | no | a person decides on every form |
| Systematic monitoring | no | the activity log records actions in the service, for accountability, not behaviour |
| Special categories or highly personal data | no | professional contact data; special categories are excluded by the terms |
| Large scale | no | a few projects, tens of users |
| Matching or combining datasets | no | |
| Vulnerable data subjects | no | professionals |
| Innovative technology | partly | artificial intelligence reads documents, but it does not process personal data about persons as its object |
| Processing that prevents people from exercising a right or using a service | no | |

## Result

One criterion is partly met; the guidelines suggest an assessment where two or more are. An impact assessment is not required for now.

## Look again when

- the artificial intelligence starts to process personal data as its object (for example reading names or signatures to match people);
- the service grows to many organisations or thousands of users;
- the activity log or analytics start to be used to evaluate users;
- any new kind of data is collected.
